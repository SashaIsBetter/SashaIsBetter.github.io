12:T5e0,<h2>Overthinking is the default</h2>
<p>A 9B reasoning model will happily spend thousands of tokens on a question that needed ten. On a laptop that&#39;s not just slow, it&#39;s heat and battery.</p>
<h2>Three routes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Small talk</strong> gets no reasoning at all.</li>
<li><strong>Quantitative questions</strong> get two quick drafts without reasoning. If they agree, that agreement is the check and the answer stands. If they disagree, Noetic reasons with twice the usual budget.</li>
<li><strong>Everything else</strong>, including anything that asks Noetic to make or run something, reasons with tools available.</li>
</ul>
<h2>A budget, not a wall</h2>
<p>Reasoning is capped (1,536 tokens on Balanced). When the budget runs out, Qwen&#39;s own published transition nudges the model to wrap up and answer, rather than cutting it off mid-thought.</p>
<h2>Checking the answer</h2>
<p>Math answers are recomputed by a short program, and code is tested, both in a sandbox. If a check disagrees, the answer is corrected before the user sees it. Checks are capped at 30 seconds, because a slow check is worse than none.</p>
<h2>A lesson about sampling</h2>
<p>Following a published recommendation, we used a presence penalty of 1.5. On MLX it is applied to the last 64 tokens, which penalises spaces and common words, and it occasionally produced garbled, mixed-language text. We replaced it with min-p 0.05, which removes the near-zero-probability tail without dulling the model.</p>
