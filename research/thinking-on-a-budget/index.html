Thinking on a budget: adaptive reasoning for a laptop model

Sasha Babenkov and the NoeticAI team · Oct 7, 2026

Small reasoning models overthink. Noetic decides per question whether to think at all, tries two quick drafts before reasoning, caps its reasoning, and checks math and code by running them.

Overthinking is the default

A 9B reasoning model will happily spend thousands of tokens on a question that needed ten. On a laptop that's not just slow, it's heat and battery.

Three routes

  • Small talk gets no reasoning at all.
  • Quantitative questions get two quick drafts without reasoning. If they agree, that agreement is the check and the answer stands. If they disagree, Noetic reasons with twice the usual budget.
  • Everything else, including anything that asks Noetic to make or run something, reasons with tools available.

A budget, not a wall

Reasoning is capped (1,536 tokens on Balanced). When the budget runs out, Qwen's own published transition nudges the model to wrap up and answer, rather than cutting it off mid-thought.

Checking the answer

Math answers are recomputed by a short program, and code is tested, both in a sandbox. If a check disagrees, the answer is corrected before the user sees it. Checks are capped at 30 seconds, because a slow check is worse than none.

A lesson about sampling

Following a published recommendation, we used a presence penalty of 1.5. On MLX it is applied to the last 64 tokens, which penalises spaces and common words, and it occasionally produced garbled, mixed-language text. We replaced it with min-p 0.05, which removes the near-zero-probability tail without dulling the model.
